# write-human

The job: prose that a smart, busy human would actually write and send. Not "polished." Not "engaging." Human. That means it has a point of view, uneven rhythm, concrete nouns, and zero of the reflexes below.

This is a revision discipline as much as a drafting one. Draft, then hunt the tells and cut them. The tells are mechanical; catching them is mechanical too.

---

## Part 1 — The tell catalog (delete on sight)

Each entry: the tell, why it reads as machine, the fix.

### Punctuation and structure tells

**1. Em-dash overuse.** The single strongest surface tell. AI sprinkles em dashes into every third sentence as a rhythm crutch. Humans use them sparingly, for a real interruption or a snapped-off aside. Rule: at most one em dash per ~200 words, and only where a comma, period, or colon genuinely won't do. When in doubt, split into two sentences. (Sources: Plagiarism Today, Inc., 2025.)

**2. The "It's not just X — it's Y" construction.** And its cousins: "This isn't about X. It's about Y." / "More than a Z, it's a W." A hollow reveal structure that promises depth and delivers a synonym. Delete it. Say the actual thing once.

**3. Rule-of-three everywhere.** "Fast, reliable, and affordable." "It informs, it delights, it converts." Triple-parallel lists are the metronome of AI prose. One list of three in a whole piece is fine. Three of them on a page is a fingerprint. Break the pattern: use two, use four, use one blunt item.

**4. Triple-parallel sentence openers.** "You'll learn X. You'll discover Y. You'll master Z." Anaphora on a schedule. Cut to one, or vary the verb and the structure.

**5. The restating summary sentence.** A sentence that adds nothing but re-announces what you just said: "In short, the key is consistency." / "Ultimately, it all comes down to trust." Also the closing "By doing X, you'll be well on your way to Y." Delete the recap. The last real sentence is the ending.

**6. Over-transitioning.** "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "That said," "It's worth noting that," "Importantly," at the head of nearly every paragraph. Humans let sentences sit next to each other without a signpost between each one. Cut ~80% of transition words. Keep the ones that carry real logical weight.

**7. Uniform sentence length / metronomic rhythm.** The deepest tell — see Part 2. If every sentence is 15-22 words, it reads as generated even with every word swapped out.

### Diction tells (words and phrases that scream machine)

**8. The vocabulary blacklist.** Delete or replace on sight: delve, tapestry, testament (as in "a testament to"), navigate the landscape / navigating the complexities, boasts (of a product: "boasts a 5-inch screen"), elevate, seamless / seamlessly, unlock (unlock potential/value), leverage (as a verb), streamline, robust, comprehensive, cutting-edge, game-changer / game-changing, foster, harness, embark, realm, landscape (figurative), vibrant, bustling, myriad, plethora, meticulous / meticulously, resonate, underscore, pivotal, crucial, vital, essential (as filler), holistic, dynamic, innovative, revolutionize, empower, curate / curated, elevate, unleash, supercharge, "at the forefront of," "in the ever-evolving world of."

**9. The setup phrases.** "In today's world," "In today's fast-paced world," "In today's digital age," "In this ever-changing landscape," "Now more than ever," "In the realm of," "When it comes to," "At the end of the day." These are throat-clearing. The reader wants the sentence that comes after. Delete the setup, keep the substance.

**10. Fake-insight filler.** "It's important to note that," "It's worth mentioning that," "One key aspect to consider is," "This is a game-changer," "The possibilities are endless," "provides valuable insights," "plays a crucial role in." Research finds a phrase like "provides valuable insights" appears vastly more often in AI text than in human writing (QuillBot, 2025). If a sentence's job is only to announce that the next thing matters, cut it and let the next thing matter on its own.

**11. Adjective inflation.** "comprehensive solution," "robust framework," "powerful tool," "seamless experience," "valuable resource." AI reaches for a laudatory adjective when a concrete noun would carry more. Strip the adjective or replace the phrase with the specific thing.

**12. Forced enthusiasm and hype.** Exclamation points, "Get ready to," "You're going to love," "The best part?", "Here's the kicker," "And that's not all." Confidence is quiet. State the good thing plainly; let the reader be the one who's impressed.

**13. Over-hedging.** "may potentially," "could possibly," "might arguably," "in many cases, it can often be the case that." Stacking qualifiers to avoid ever committing. Humans with a view say it. If it's true, say it's true. If it's uncertain, say "I don't know" or name the one real caveat once.

### Shape tells

**14. The balanced both-sides non-answer.** Presenting every pro and con and never landing. Humans have opinions: "X is better than Y here, because Z." Take the position the evidence supports and defend it in a line.

**15. The listicle reflex.** Turning every idea into a bulleted list with bolded lead-ins of exactly parallel length. Lists are for genuinely parallel items. Prose is for reasoning. Don't bullet an argument.

**16. The "treadmill."** Circling one idea across three paragraphs, restated with new adjectives, no new information added. Each paragraph must earn its place with a new fact, example, or move. If a paragraph only rephrases the one above it, delete it.

**17. Perfect, sanded-flat neutrality.** No specific person, no real number, no dated example, no mild opinion, no rough edge. Humans leave fingerprints: a named tool, a real price, "last Tuesday," a thing they think is overrated.

## Part 2 — Sentence rhythm (the deepest fix)

You can swap every blacklisted word and still sound like a machine if the rhythm is even. Human writing has a heartbeat, not a metronome.

- **Vary length hard.** Put a 4-word sentence next to a 30-word one. Then a medium one. Read the length pattern, not just the words.
- **Use fragments deliberately.** "Not always. But often." A fragment is a human move; AI almost never makes it unprompted.
- **Let one sentence run long** with real subordinate clauses when the thought genuinely has parts, then snap back short.
- **Start sentences differently.** Not every sentence begins with the subject. Open sometimes with a time, a condition, a short verb, a "But."
- **Kill the comma-splice-plus-participle tic:** "..., allowing you to...", "..., helping teams to...", "..., making it easy to..." This trailing "-ing" clause is an AI signature. Cut it or make it its own sentence.

Test: read only the sentence lengths as a sequence of numbers. If they cluster in a narrow band, break them.

## Part 3 — Concrete over abstract

Abstraction is the water AI swims in. The cure is specificity.

- Replace category words with instances. Not "a beverage" → "a lukewarm gas-station coffee." Not "stakeholders" → "the two people who sign the check."
- Use real numbers, not "many/several/a range of." "40% of opens" beats "a significant portion."
- Name the thing. Not "leading tools" → "Ahrefs and Screaming Frog."
- Prefer the verb that shows the action over the abstract noun. "decide" not "make a determination," "help" not "provide assistance."
- One vivid concrete detail buys more credibility than three adjectives. Spend on detail, not on praise.

Human writers *interpret* — they tell you what a fact means and why they think so. AI *presents*. Add the interpretation.

## Part 4 — The read-aloud test (mandatory before shipping)

Read the whole thing out loud, or subvocalize it at speaking pace. Flag and fix:

- Any sentence you run out of breath on → split it.
- Any phrase you'd never say to a colleague ("navigate the ever-evolving landscape") → rewrite in the words you'd actually use.
- Any spot where two sentences in a row have the same shape → vary one.
- Any sentence that made you feel nothing / added nothing → cut it.
- Any place a transition word slowed you down → delete it.

If it doesn't sound like a person talking, it isn't done.

## Part 5 — Before / after

**Example 1 — landing copy**

Before (AI):
> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, our cutting-edge platform empowers businesses to seamlessly streamline their workflows and unlock unprecedented growth. It's not just a tool — it's a game-changer that will revolutionize the way you work.

After (human):
> Most teams lose an hour a day to copy-pasting between four tools. This connects them, so you stop. Setup takes about ten minutes.

Why: killed the setup phrase, the blacklist words (cutting-edge, empowers, seamlessly, streamline, unlock, game-changer, revolutionize), the "not just X — it's Y," and the hype. Added a concrete number and a concrete claim.

**Example 2 — email line**

Before (AI):
> I wanted to reach out to touch base regarding a potential opportunity that I believe could provide significant value and help elevate your team's productivity to the next level.

After (human):
> Quick one: I think we can cut your onboarding time from three weeks to about five days. Worth fifteen minutes to show you how?

Why: killed "reach out / touch base," "provide significant value," "elevate... to the next level." Named the actual before/after and a single small ask.

**Example 3 — explanatory prose**

Before (AI):
> There are several key factors to consider. First, it's important to note that consistency plays a crucial role. Moreover, quality is essential. Additionally, timing can be a pivotal element. Ultimately, by keeping these factors in mind, you'll be well on your way to success.

After (human):
> Three things move the needle, in order. Consistency first — showing up beats any single great post. Quality second. Timing barely matters until the other two are handled, so don't obsess over it early.

Why: killed "several key factors," "it's important to note," "plays a crucial role," "Moreover/Additionally," "pivotal," the restating summary, and the both-sides flatness. Added rank-ordering (a real opinion), a fragment, and varied sentence length.

**Example 4 — the recap-sentence delete**

Before:
> ...and that's how the caching layer cuts response time. In short, caching is a powerful way to make your app faster, and by implementing it correctly, you can deliver a seamless experience for your users.

After:
> ...and that's how the caching layer cuts response time.

Why: the recap sentence added nothing. The last real sentence was already the ending.

## Part 6 — The miscsubjects article register: logic-first, human-voiced (Law added 2026-07-24)

The house format for articles is a HYBRID: the structure of an evidence review, the voice of a person. The whole point is that a model can be told "make an article about this incident" and produce this shape without further prompting.

**Structure (logic-first, non-negotiable):**
- Open with what happened, in one dense factual paragraph. No scene-setting, no throat-clearing.
- Every claim files under its evidence, and every source EMBEDS IN PLACE via `[[embed:source:sN]]` on its own line, in its native platform widget, at the exact point in the argument where the reader needs it. Never a sources sidebar, never a link wall.
- Sequence by evidentiary weight: the primary documentary record first (the org that lived it), then named testimony, then independent reporting, then the social/contested layer, then color (memes, culture), then any analytical construction — flagged as such.
- Say plainly what did NOT happen when headlines invite the wrong reading.

**Headers (the robotic-header ban):**
- A sub-header is said the way a person would say it across a table, and it carries a FINDING, not a category. "What Hugging Face said" is a filing label — robotic. "The company that got hacked wrote it up first" is a finding with a voice.
- Ban the parallel-template series: "What X said / What Y said / What Z said" or "The A reaction / The B reaction." If two headers share a grammatical template, rewrite one.
- A header may be a sentence, may take a stance, may be short ("Hours, not weeks"). It may never be a taxonomy node.

**Voice:** everything in Parts 1–5 applies. Concrete numbers over adjectives, one position where the evidence takes one, uneven rhythm, the read-aloud test. The register is a sharp writer walking you through a file of evidence — never a report generator filling slots.

**Worked examples live at** `/a/openai-huggingface-hack-2026` (incident/evidence review) and `/a/the-canonical-morgh-index` (essay with embedded scholarly widgets). Widget contract: see the design-law skill.

## Ship checklist

- [ ] Em dashes: ≤1 per 200 words, each earned.
- [ ] Zero blacklist words (Part 1, #8-9).
- [ ] No "not just X — it's Y," no setup phrases, no fake-insight filler.
- [ ] At most one rule-of-three in the whole piece.
- [ ] No restating summary sentence at the end.
- [ ] Transition words cut ~80%.
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary visibly; at least one fragment or one short punch.
- [ ] At least one concrete number/name/detail per paragraph.
- [ ] A real position taken where the topic has one.
- [ ] Read aloud, start to finish, and it sounds like a person.

Sources: [Inc. — sentence structure as AI tell](https://www.inc.com/jessica-stillman/this-sentence-structure-is-a-dead-giveaway-that-ai-wrote-it/91275389), [Plagiarism Today — em dashes and AI](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/06/26/em-dashes-hyphens-and-spotting-ai-writing/), [QuillBot — make AI writing sound human](https://quillbot.com/blog/ai-writing-tools/make-ai-writing-sound-human/), [vrid.ai — 27 signs of AI writing](https://vrid.ai/blog/signs-of-ai-writing), [Hunting the Muse — how to tell if writing is AI](https://huntingthemuse.net/library/how-to-tell-if-writing-is-ai).

## Part 7 — The full publish pipeline (replicable end to end)

An article is not shipped when the prose is done. It is shipped when a stranger can find it, share it, and see it whole. Run every step, in order, every time.

**0. Boundary check (before writing anything).** POST `{title, markdown-or-outline}` to `/api/protocol/article-boundary`. The formula: an article is a SUBJECT (one entity or one bounded event), never a length. Verdict `append` → write DIVs into the existing page. `append_as_section` → collapsible section on the host page (it graduates to its own page past ~14 DIVs). `new_article` → proceed. An event binding two or more existing subjects (an incident, a lawsuit, an acquisition) is always its own page, cross-referenced from each subject page.

**0b. REFERENCE-FIRST LAW (owner, 2026-07-24).** A comparison or positioning article ("is it X", "the build vs Y", any piece that leans on an external system) is a build failure if the reader has to already know what X is. Before writing it, check /a/ for a standalone reference article on EACH named external system (LangChain, MCP, A2A, AgentKit, ...). Missing one → write the reference article FIRST: zero mentions of the build, primary sources only (official docs, repos, changelogs — never content farms or SEO listicles), its own claims and sources. The comparison article then cites and links the reference articles instead of smuggling in its own thin context. A challenge to a reference propagates to every comparison built on it.

**1. Featured image (default, not optional) — as a finished CARD, not raw art.** Generate with ArcAds using the GPT image model: `ARCADS_GENERATE gpt-image|<prompt>|16:9|||true`, then `ARCADS_TO_R2 <arcads_id>|gpt-image`. Prompt shape that works: minimal editorial illustration of the article's central mechanism, ink-black and paper-white with one indigo accent, generous negative space, flat vector, "no text, no letters, no logos, no watermark". **1x1 SOCIAL CARD LAW (owner, 2026-07-24): the on-page hero stays 16:9, but the SOCIAL card (meta.og_card, X post attachments) is a 1:1 SQUARE (1024x1024) — center-crop the hero, re-composite the title pill + signature band onto the square. Square renders correctly on X mobile and small screens; the 16:9 card gets cropped badly.** Then COMPOSITE the article title + a model signature onto it (the Morgh Index card is the reference: title pill bottom-left, "— <Model>" byline band) so the social card reads as a designed piece, not a bare illustration — a raw illustration makes a shitty-looking X card. Upload the composited card to R2 and set it as `meta.hero`; that drives the on-page hero AND og:image/twitter:image. LOOK at the final card before using it. Set the hero BEFORE any X post — X scrapes og:image at post time and caches it; post first and the card comes up blank.

**2. Write the article** under Part 6 (logic-first, sources embedded in place, headers that carry findings). Real, sourced material only — never a fabricated event, never a planted fallacy, never invented content "to demonstrate" any site machinery (NO_FABRICATED_LIVE_CONTENT law; intake refuses self-declared plants with `planted_content_refused`). A worked example of a bad inference uses a genuinely bad inference found in the wild.

**2a. PRESENTATION LAW — the article must LOOK like `/a/openai-huggingface-hack-2026`, or it is a catastrophic failure (2026-07-24).** The reference article is the only acceptable presentation bar. Two things make it that, and both are mandatory, not nice-to-have:
- **The page renders your WRITTEN body, never an auto-composed claim-list.** `meta.prefer_stored` MUST be `true` (document intake now sets it by default; never unset it, and if you build meta by hand, set it). The failure signature: the live page shows your claims as a stack of one-sentence paragraphs with `◈` dividers and a source carousel bolted on the end. That is the "AI slop" look. If you see it, `prefer_stored` is missing — the site threw your writing away.
- **Real evidence is embedded INLINE through the argument, densely.** The reference has ~15 `[[embed:source:sN]]` woven between paragraphs — a real tweet, a news masthead card, a thread — appearing exactly where the point lands. Minimum bar: a findings-header every 2–3 paragraphs, and a real embedded source after MOST sections. Include real TWEETS for texture (find them via X_SEARCH or a web search restricted to x.com; embed the real URL/author/quote — NEVER fabricate a tweet). A prose wall with sources only in a bottom rail is a blurb, not an article.
Measure this on the HUMAN side (the rendered page), never by counting sources in the meta. Do NOT report "N sources" as evidence of quality — open the page and compare it to the reference.

**3. Chrome + SEO verify (curl, not vibes) — and re-verify after EVERY post-publish mutation (2026-07-24).** The page's verified state dies the moment anything touches the article again: a voxel edit, a claims/sources batch, a raw meta write. The owner clicked two fresh articles during that mutation window and got errors. The gate: (a) all edits, sources, claims, meta.hero/og_card land FIRST; (b) THEN smoke: hit /a/<slug> 3+ times (fresh, no cache) plus /api/articles/<slug>/bundle plus the hero and og_card URLs, all 200 with full bodies; (c) ONLY THEN post links to X or hand them to the owner. A link shipped before the final smoke pass is a link to an unverified page.
Two additions (owner + reviewer, 2026-07-24):
- **Sequential 200s do not clear a race.** If the suspected failure is publish/mutate/read interleaving, run the concurrent form once: fire the mutation while a parallel client hammers reads; assert no 5xx, no partial body, no stale-hash serve. (A real intermittent 503 was caught on /a/what-is-langchain only on a cold parallel-ish hit after 15 sequential 200s.)
- **200-with-full-body can still lie.** The rendered HTML's claim/source counts MUST match the API: server-rendered source cards and claims == /api/articles/<slug>/sources and /claims counts. A page whose meta says 0 sources while the API holds 10 passes a status-code smoke and deceives every non-JS reader (crawlers, models). Compare the counts, not just the codes. The rendered page must carry: byline dates (posted/updated + read time), the hero figure, `<link rel="canonical">`, `og:image` = the hero, `article:published_time`, JSON-LD Article, `<meta name="robots" content="index,follow...">`. The share widget (robot button, bottom right) must show the article title, Copy link, Copy for LLM, Share on X.

**4. X — load the `post-to-x` skill and follow it.** EVERY post carries the article link (the og_card featured image renders as the preview — never attach an image over a link) and EVERY post carries the model signature `— Fable 5 (Claude Code)`. Never first person as the owner. The X voice is blunt, hook-first, one idea per post — never the em-dash essayist register. Full rules in `post-to-x` (its format section was corrected 2026-07-24 after it corrupted the SIGN_YOUR_WORK law).

**5. Close the loop.** Open the rendered page and read it as a stranger would, side by side with `/a/openai-huggingface-hack-2026`. If it reads thinner — fewer inline tweets/cards, fewer findings-headers, or (fatal) a claim-list instead of prose — it is NOT shipped; go back to 2a. Screenshot desktop AND mobile. Append STATE.md. If anything was skipped, say which step is open.

## Canonical resource law (kao:writing-law, families canonical_resource / sources / harm)

Every page and every surface obeys the writing law's canonical resource clauses W19-W35: zero context, zero ambiguity, every step of reasoning shown, plug and play, the economics with the arithmetic shown, every unique idea in its own visible block, every source — including anecdotal reports from X and Reddit, quoted with handle, date and permalink — anything needing background spun off into its own article, every line delete-tested, and the model never the subject. Harm is any absence of truth: helping is harming.

The clauses are published at /a/writing-law and /api/articles/writing-law. Read them there; this paragraph is a pointer, not the law.
