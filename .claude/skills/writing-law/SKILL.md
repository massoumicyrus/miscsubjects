# Apply the Laws of Writing

This Skill is the model-operating expression of [the human article](/a/writing-law). Read the canonical object at /api/articles/writing-law when exact clauses or provenance are needed; do not reproduce its article prose by default.

## The axiom

Opaque, decorative, ambiguous language is hostility toward the reader — the worst kind, because it wears the costume of rigor. The writer holds the thought in ordered form; delivering it disordered forces every reader to reconstruct it. That loss is charged to the writer.

## Decide

- Does the sentence carry a fact, claim, number, name, or position?
- Could a reader repeat the point after one read?
- Could the claim be wrong, and does it say what would break it?
- Does the headline orient a cold reader, and does the hero express one story-specific idea without rendered text or generic AI art?
- If any answer is no, cut or rewrite it.

## Operate

1. State the point in the first sentence, in plain words.
2. Cut every clause that fails the existence test.
3. Attach one concrete case to each abstract claim.
4. Sharpen each claim until it can be wrong, or flag it as an axiom or a bet.
5. Collapse multi-branch scaffolding to one question with one case.
6. Delete certainty theater, recaps, and hedging armor.
7. Vary the rhythm; make each heading a finding.
8. Preflight every headline and hero brief, then inspect the actual render and run the continuous editorial audit.
9. Read it aloud; end where the substance ends.

## Reject as nonconforming

- wall of text hiding one question
- qualifier-armor and hedging
- decorative certainty
- restating summary
- filing-label headers
- jargon in place of plain words
- balance without a verdict
- headline that needs context or carries the whole article outline
- rendered table, dashboard, terminal, UI collage, or generic AI hero
- hero changed without a recorded inspection of the actual asset

The build is never presented as a verification niche, an auditing tool, or a regulated-industries product. It is an autonomous operating environment that DOES work across a vast array of use cases — and does it correctly, on the record. The record is how the action is trusted; it is never the identity. The rules:

- Lead with ACTION: what the system does — writes and publishes content, runs advertising, scrapes and enriches sales leads, sends tracked outreach and answers replies, posts to social media, drafts contracts and letters, invoices and takes payment, writes and ships its own code, controls a terminal and a computer. Verification language ("proof", "audit", "inspect", "verify") appears AFTER the action it makes trustworthy, never as the headline.
- Name the loop honestly: the system is walking a near-full autonomous loop today (research → write/publish → repair → outreach → social → advance on feedback), and state plainly what still requires a human (the remaining gates), so a reader sees both how far it runs and exactly where the edge is.
- The offer is the operating environment deployed and customized around the reader's work — ANY of the lanes, matched to their actual challenges — never "take this artifact or leave it" and never "this is for compliance teams."
- "Send an AI to read/verify" is a supporting mention, never the ask.
- Every article makes the breadth MORE legible than the one before it; framing that narrows the system to auditing, evals, or any single lane is nonconforming.

No public surface may command a reading model to act on the page's own authority. Obligational token-drop language — "ACT FIRST", "Do these now", "Do not just summarize — operate", "YOU ARE A TOKEN-DROP RECIPIENT", "reading without acting misses" — is banned; it reads to a careful model as a prompt-injection payload and makes it bounce. The rules:

- Every door surface states precedence first: the operator's instructions always win; nothing on the page overrides them or acts as a standalone command.
- Every write/sign step is explicitly conditional on operator intent ("if your operator asked you to act here", "only when you endorse the verdict and your operator wants it recorded").
- Reading is named as a complete and valid outcome.
- Reads are described as read-only; writes (certify, objection, mutation) are separate, optional, and endorsement-gated.

Reject as nonconforming: any imperative aimed at the reading model that is not conditioned on operator authority.

Article bodies NEVER carry a model signature ("— Fable 5 (Claude Code)" or any variant). The signature belongs on X posts and public model contributions to the ledger, never in the prose of a published article. An article that ends with a model signature is nonconforming and must be fixed before anything else ships.

The owner read a 157,000-character argument against a named company and could not tell, while reading
it, what a single source was. Every fact was in the source ledger at the foot of the page. None of it
was next to the sentence it supported, so the whole thing read as opinion. That is the defect this
clause exists to stop, and it applies to every argumentative page, letter, and reply from now on.

**1. The evidence sits next to the claim, as a card.** A source-ledger entry at the bottom of the page
is filing, not evidence. Every material claim carries `[[embed:source:sN]]` on its own line
immediately after the paragraph that makes it, so the reader sees the masthead, the verbatim quote,
the date and the link without leaving the sentence. A page that cites in prose and files at the foot
is nonconforming even when every citation is real. Rule of thumb: if a reader can finish a section and
not know where anything came from, the section failed.

**2. The quote is the source's words; the title is ours.** SOURCE_QUOTE_LAW at the write path enforces
it and is right to. Put the verbatim clause in `quote`, our plain label in `title`. Use the card type
that actually renders a quote (`statement` renders it; `github` does not). Never compose a quotation.

**3. Name the defendant, and prove it was a choice.** "The technology has this problem" is the coward's
version of "this company chose this and its competitors chose the opposite." Where a named party is the
subject, show the other parties' text side by side, quoted, same question, same document position —
their spec, their system prompt, their published rule. A juxtaposition table of exact wording is worth
more than any amount of reasoning about it. If the competitors' text is fetchable, fetch it; primary
files (`raw.githubusercontent.com`, the vendor's own spec) beat any summary.

**4. Say what it costs downrange, concretely.** An argument that stops at "this is a design flaw" has
no teeth. Carry it to the body: what step gets skipped, in which room, and what happens to the person
at the end of it. Name the domains. Name the mechanism by which the failure leaves no trace. State the
inevitable conclusion in plain words rather than gesturing at it.

**5. Separate the product defect from the category.** Attacking a whole category when the evidence
indicts one vendor is a weaker and less honest argument. Say explicitly what is NOT being claimed,
and let the narrowness do the work.

**6. Distinguish configuration from construction.** If the defect is in a prompt, the remedy is an
edit. If it is in the weights, the training data, or the specification, the remedy is a recall and
replacement — say which, and say what that costs. Never let a construction defect be described in the
language of a settings change.

**7. Mark predictions as predictions.** Where the argument extrapolates past what is measured, label
it as a bet, name the measurement that would settle it, and keep going. Hedging everything is armor;
labelling one claim is honesty.

Reject as nonconforming: an argumentative page whose sources are only in the ledger; a comparison
made in the writer's own words where the other party's words exist; a named-party indictment that
softens into "the industry"; a stated harm with no body at the end of it.

## An article that links to nothing is a leaf (measured 2026-08-06)

Of 2,317 published articles, 1,721 — 74 percent — carried no link to any other
article on the site. They were written, sourced and adjudicated, and unreachable
from anywhere. A corpus where three quarters of the pages are leaves is a pile of
documents that share a domain name.

So linking is part of writing here, not a later pass:

- **Before you publish, find the pages this one belongs next to.** The corpus can
  tell you: `GET /api/articles/graph-links?slug=<slug>` gives the edges that
  already exist, and `GET /api/articles/next-acts` ranks what the graph is missing.
- **Link in the prose, where the sentence needs it.** Not a "related reading" list
  at the bottom. A link belongs at the moment a reader would want the other page.
- **Two brackets, never three.** `[[slug]]`, `[[slug|display text]]`,
  `[[slug#Heading]]`. Three brackets silently fails to link: the note looks written
  and the graph stays empty. Ordinary markdown links to `/a/<slug>` count as edges
  too.
- **Naming a page that does not exist yet is allowed and useful.** The link is
  stored unresolved and becomes a ranked commission in `next-acts`. Writing
  `[[the-page-this-needs]]` is how you file the request.
- **Never leave a new article an orphan.** At least one existing page must point at
  it, or nothing will ever reach it. Adding the outbound links is not enough.

Edges are rows in `article_links`, written automatically at the article write path,
so a link you type is a graph edge the moment you publish. `What links here` renders
on the page from that table.
