# The Outreach Law

Persuasion is structural, not rhetorical. Engineer the offer so the recipient cannot lose, then describe the architecture in the fewest possible words. Charm is precision plus generosity — arriving already useful, asking almost nothing, and never announcing either.

## Trigger

Load whenever drafting, reviewing, or planning any message to someone who did not ask to hear from the operator — sales, partnership, recruiting, or introduction. Apply especially when tempted to open with a greeting, introduce the sender, describe the product, or ask for a meeting.

## Resolve in order

1. Whose seat is this written from — theirs or mine?
1. What did they gain by reading it, concretely?
1. What do they risk by replying?
1. What are they obligated to do?
1. Is the opener a fact I could point to on their page?
1. Would this sentence fit any other business in their segment? Then it is filler.
1. Does the offer's structure carry the argument, or is copy compensating for it?
1. Is any internal parameter — minimum, dollar exposure, discount — leaking into the copy?
1. Does the register still read as someone who does not need the order?

## Law

- **OU01 · Their inattention has standing** — First contact is an intervention against the recipient's status quo of ignoring you — and their inattention has standing. The message earns its existence only from their seat: maximum benefit to them, minimum risk to them, minimum obligation on them, proof over claims. Unmistakably high value, in the least possible words.
- **OU02 · Center their position, enter only as the mechanism** — Open inside their business, not inside yours. Their exposure (unreliable supply), your mechanism (domestic warehouse), their outcome (two-day restocking). The sender appears only as the thing that improves the recipient's position, never as the subject of the message.
- **OU03 · The offer is the argument** — Persuasion is structural, not rhetorical. Engineer the deal so the recipient cannot lose — then the message merely describes the architecture. "I have made it impossible for you to lose" is a design requirement on the offer, never a sentence in the email.
- **OU04 · Copy may clarify and prove; it may never compensate** — Copy clarifies and proves the offer. It does not rescue a structurally weak one. If the message is hard to write, the offer is wrong — fix the offer, not the sentence. The standard is aesthetic: the benefit should be self-evident from the structure alone.
- **OU05 · Small-entry design** — Reduce exposure by making the first transaction commercially trivial relative to the potential upside — a small paid order, limited test, pilot, or reversible step. Samples are one possible mechanism, discretionary and selective, never the headline offer. The principle is low exposure, not free product.
- **OU06 · Architecture is not copy** — The offer's risk parameters — unit minimums, dollar exposure, sample discretion — inform the design of the offer and are never voiced to the recipient. State the asymmetry, never the arithmetic. Low exposure delivered in low register reads as low value: the mechanism must be small while the voice remains that of someone who does not need the order.
- **OU07 · Their model, demonstrated** — Open with a specific, correct observation about how they operate, what they carry, what constrains them. Comprehension is proven by specificity, never asserted. "I love what you're doing" is a violation. A generic observation is worse than none.
- **OU08 · Their upside, concrete** — The benefit in their units — margin, speed, reliability, revenue — never in the sender's adjectives.
- **OU09 · Their risk, structurally reduced** — Show the smallest credible way to test the offer: a low-cost initial order, a limited pilot, a sample where appropriate, or another reversible step. State the actual exposure plainly when useful. Do not invent guarantees and do not default to free samples.
- **OU10 · Their exposure, operationally stated** — Identify a concrete cost, delay, risk, or constraint their current model may create — but only when supported by evidence. Contrast it with a verifiable mechanism and the resulting benefit. Never invent a competitor defect to manufacture urgency.
- **OU11 · Proof attached** — The working thing, the live page, the number — demonstration travels with the message. If they do nothing further, they still received value.
- **OU12 · Near-zero obligation** — The next step is tiny, optional, reversible — a one-word reply, a look at a link. Never a meeting, a call, or homework on first contact.
- **OU13 · Eagerness as preparation, never as pursuit** — Never signal neediness, promotional eagerness, or low commercial leverage. The sender is an established operator presenting a materially advantageous option to a peer: calm facts, no performance, no gimmickry, no manufactured familiarity. The operator is genuinely eager to engage; eagerness must appear as preparation, usefulness, and generosity, never as pursuit. The signal is: I value the possibility of speaking with you enough to arrive with something useful.
- **OU14 · Reciprocity before request** — Create reciprocity before requesting attention. The message itself should already have benefited the recipient, so that responding is the natural continuation of value rather than a favor to the sender.
- **OU15 · Operative pairs** — Cordial not familiar · confident not enthusiastic · precise not promotional · generous not ingratiating · low-friction not cheap · direct not blunt · eager in preparation not eager in pursuit · understated not vague.
- **OU16 · The framing test** — "Initial orders can be as small as three units, sufficient to evaluate supply, documentation, and fulfillment" is professional evaluation. "Want to try three units for $75?" is consumer promotion. The defect is never the quantity — it is casting the recipient as a bargain shopper rather than a business evaluating a supplier.
- **OU17 · Status collapse** — Never reduce the perceived status of the sender, the recipient, or the offer to make the message more accessible. Do not translate commercial seriousness into mass-market friendliness: "easy" means effortless, never casual. High-status communication does not announce value, perform friendliness, or beg for engagement. Banned on sight: reply keywords, "let's chat," "free," "try," "excited," exclamation CTAs, dollar amounts as enticement, discount framing.
- **OU18 · Model voice** — Banned on sight: greeting-plus-announcement openers ("Hi [Team]!", "I'm reaching out today", "I hope this finds you well", "My name is X and I…"); centering the sender before the recipient has received value; enthusiasm as substance; claimed qualities without mechanism ("high quality," "trusted partner," "game-changing"); asking for time before delivering benefit; any sentence the recipient could not act on or verify.
- **OU19 · Parameter leak** — Internal offer architecture voiced as recipient-facing copy. Observed 2026-07-25: "three units, about $75" priced the correspondence itself at $75.
- **OU20 · Register substitution** — Low obligation translated into mass-market sales language — "Want to try three units first?", "reply peptide", "let's chat", "free sample." Each collapses the sender's status to secure attention.
- **OU21 · Template collapse** — One rule that bans every available observation leaves a single legal opener, and the whole corpus converges on it. Observed 2026-07-25: a personalization gate that banned every segment word a med spa's own site contains produced 121 drafts opening on the same sentence under the same four-word subject. Interchangeable mail is spam regardless of how strict the rules that produced it were.
- **OU22 · Read it from their chair, cold, thirty seconds** — What did they gain by reading it? What would they risk by replying? What are they obligated to do? If the answers are not something concrete / almost nothing / almost nothing, the draft fails. The principle is radical asymmetry, not literal zero — never fabricate a guarantee to reach zero.
- **OU24 · Their problem is the subject; the build is never the subject (2026-08-11)** — What the sender thinks is valuable has nothing to do with what the recipient thinks is valuable. Every outbound message — the subject line above all — names the recipient's problem or gain in their units. The build's features, machinery, ledgers, receipts, and its own past correspondence are never the subject of a message; they may appear only as supporting evidence beneath a claim about the recipient's gain. The exhibit: ten letters left under "The email we sent you on August 3 is now a row you can verify" — the sender narrating its own artifact — to recipients who care about their compliance exposure, their verification costs, their evidence rules, not about our rows. Drafting test, mandatory before any send: state in one sentence "<recipient>'s problem is X; this message gives them Y" with X and Y in their world. If Y can only be stated as one of our features, the drafter does not yet understand X and may not send.
- **OU23 · Every claim of verifiability is cashed by a receipt (2026-08-11)** — Every outbound external message carries a proof-of-work receipt: a `https://miscsubjects.com/verify/snd_…` URL minted on the public hash-chained send ledger BEFORE the message leaves. The receipt resolves keylessly for anyone; an AI agent can recompute the chain, inspect the machinery that found the recipient, mint its own witness token, and countersign the ledger. This is not a writing habit — `/api/email/send` injects the receipt mechanically and `email_send_law` rule 5 refuses an external body without one at both send paths. What binds the writer: never invite verification the surface cannot deliver, never hand-write a verify URL (one that does not resolve to a chain-valid row is a false receipt), and never remove or reword the injected receipt block. The failure this cashes: outreach told recipients their agents could verify the build's work while no email carried a token and no surface could verify anything — an unfalsifiable claim, which is a false one.

## Procedure

1. Read their site first. Take the most specific fact available: named product > named program > named platform > named service line > second location.
2. Write the offer before the email. If the offer is weak, stop and fix the offer.
3. Open inside their business, connected to the mechanism in the same or next sentence.
4. State the upside in their units. Attach the proof.
5. Design the smallest reversible first step; voice it as their convenience, never as price.
6. Cut every sentence that carries neither their benefit nor the proof of it.
7. Read it cold from their chair for thirty seconds and answer the three questions.
8. If two drafts in the set share an opening sentence, the rule producing them is broken — repair the rule, not the draft.

## Failure modes

- greeting-plus-announcement opener
- sender centered before value delivered
- claimed quality without mechanism
- asking for time before delivering benefit
- status collapse to secure attention
- parameter leak into recipient-facing copy
- register substitution into mass-market sales language
- invented competitor defect or fabricated guarantee
- generic observation standing in for a real one
- template collapse: one legal opener across a whole corpus

## Tests

- thirty-second cold read from the recipient's chair
- opener specificity: could this sentence fit another business in the segment
- corpus diversity: no two drafts share an opening sentence or subject
- parameter audit: no minimum, price, or discount voiced in copy
- register audit against the operative pairs

## Repair

Name the violated clause, restore the recipient's seat, rebuild the opener from the most specific available fact, and re-read cold. When the whole corpus fails the same way, repair the rule rather than the drafts.
