# business-email

The reader is a busy peer. They will decide in about three seconds whether to read the rest and in about ten whether to act. Every rule below serves those two facts.

Always run the draft through **write-human** as well — a business email is where AI tells (setup phrases, "I wanted to reach out," "circle back," em-dashes, hype adjectives) are most obvious and most damaging.

---

## Rule 1 — One idea per email

An email is one thought that needs one response. If you have three asks, you have three emails, or one email with a clearly numbered list and a single "which of these can you take?" close. Mixing a decision, an FYI, and a scheduling question buries all three and gets you a partial reply. When you catch yourself writing "Also," or "One more thing," stop and decide whether it's a separate email.

---

## Rule 2 — The subject line

The subject line decides whether the email is opened; a large share of performance traces to it alone (Cognism, Machine Marketing, 2025). Rules:

- **Say the content, not a teaser.** "Contract v3 — need your sign-off by Thu" beats "Quick question."
- **Under ~50 characters** so it survives on mobile (clients truncate at 30-40). Front-load the words that matter.
- **Put the ask or the deadline in it** when there is one: "Approval needed: Q3 budget," "Re: Monday demo — moved to 2pm."
- **Lowercase reads human, not marketing.** "quick q on the invoice" lands like a colleague, not a campaign — use it for warm/internal, keep title case for first-contact formality.
- **Never clickbait a peer.** "You won't believe this" burns trust you need for the next email.

---

## Rule 3 — Structure: context → point → single ask

The body has three moves, in this order, and short:

1. **Context (one line).** Why this email, why now, why them. "Following our call Tuesday about the migration —"
2. **The point (one to three lines).** The actual thing. State it directly. If it's a decision, state the decision. If it's a request, the request is coming but first they need the one fact that makes it reasonable.
3. **The single ask (one line, unmissable).** Exactly what you want them to do, and by when. "Can you approve the attached by Thursday?" Put it on its own line. One ask.

The whole thing fits on a phone screen without scrolling. If it doesn't, cut.

---

## Rule 4 — Brevity

- Target 50-125 words for most work email. Longer only when you're genuinely conveying dense information they asked for.
- Delete the preamble. "I hope this email finds you well" / "I wanted to reach out" / "I'm writing to" — cut all of it. Start with the context line.
- One idea per sentence. No semicolons bundling two thoughts.
- If a sentence doesn't move toward the ask or the context for it, delete it.
- Bullets only for genuinely parallel items (options, a checklist). Never bullet an argument.

---

## Rule 5 — Warm vs curt (calibration)

Match the register to the relationship and the stakes.

- **First contact / senior / external:** warm-professional. One line of genuine human acknowledgment, then business. Not effusive.
- **Established peer, routine matter:** curt is respectful. It signals you value their time. "Bill — invoice #204 is wrong, it double-charges the setup fee. Can you reissue? Thanks." That's a complete, good email.
- **Bad news / pushback / decline:** warm in tone, unambiguous in substance. Don't soften the fact into vagueness. "We're going to pass on this — the timeline doesn't work for us this quarter. If it slips to Q4, I'd want to revisit."
- **Never** fake warmth with exclamation points and "So excited!!" Warmth is specificity and respect, not enthusiasm punctuation.

Read the room: over-warm to a curt operator reads as needy; over-curt to a relationship-first contact reads as cold. When unsure, one warm line up top, business below.

---

## Rule 6 — The follow-up

- **Wait a real interval** (2-4 business days for most asks) before the first nudge.
- **Add a new reason to reply — never "just bumping this" or "circling back."** Those are noise. Add a fact, a deadline that got closer, a smaller version of the ask, or make it trivially easy: "No worries if now's not the time — should I check back after the launch, or is this a no?"
- **Give an easy out.** A yes/no follow-up ("still worth doing, or should I close this out?") gets a reply because it costs them one word.
- **Keep the thread.** Reply to your own email so the context is one scroll away.
- **Three is the ceiling** for most business asks. After that you're a problem, not a follow-up.

## Templates

**The request**
> Subject: [thing] — need [action] by [day]
>
> [Name] — following [context].
>
> [The one fact that makes the ask reasonable.]
>
> Can you [single specific action] by [day]?
>
> Thanks,
> [you]

**The intro / first contact**
> Subject: [specific reason you're writing]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [One line: how you know of them / the specific reason, real and specific.]
>
> [One line: the point — what you do / what you're proposing, concretely.]
>
> Would [small next step] make sense? Happy to [low-cost option] if easier.
>
> Best,
> [you]

**The status update (no ask)**
> Subject: [project] — [status in 3 words]
>
> [Name] — quick update, no action needed.
>
> [What's done. What's next. Any date that moved.]
>
> Flag me if anything looks off.

**The decline**
> Subject: Re: [their thing]
>
> [Name] — thanks for [their effort]. We're going to pass on this one — [the real, specific reason].
>
> [If true: the condition under which you'd reconsider.]
>
> Appreciate you thinking of us.

## Before / after

**1 — the padded request**

Before:
> Hi Sarah, I hope you're doing well! I wanted to reach out and touch base regarding the marketing report we discussed. I was wondering if you might possibly have a chance to take a look at it when you get a moment and perhaps share any thoughts you might have. Also, I had a couple of other things I wanted to run by you as well. Looking forward to hearing from you! Best regards.

After:
> Subject: Marketing report — your read by Wed?
>
> Sarah — the draft report from our Tuesday call is attached.
>
> Can you check the Q2 numbers on p.3 and flag anything wrong by Wednesday? That's the only part I'm unsure about.
>
> Thanks,
> [you]

Why: killed the preamble, "wanted to reach out / touch base," the double-hedge "might possibly," and the buried second ask. One idea, one ask, one deadline, a subject that says the ask.

**2 — the vague FYI**

Before:
> Just wanted to give everyone a quick heads up that things are progressing on the project and we're making good progress on several fronts. There are still a few items to iron out but overall we're in a good place. Will keep you posted!

After:
> Subject: Website relaunch — on track for Aug 12
>
> Team — quick status, no action needed.
>
> Design and copy are done. Dev is 80% through; one payment-integration bug left, fix expected Friday. Still on track for the Aug 12 launch.
>
> I'll flag here if the date moves.

Why: replaced "good progress on several fronts" with what's actually done, the one real blocker, and the date. An update with no facts isn't an update.

**3 — the soft no that says nothing**

Before:
> Thanks so much for sending this over! It's really interesting and there's a lot to like here. We'll definitely keep it in mind and circle back if anything changes on our end. Really appreciate it!

After:
> Subject: Re: partnership proposal
>
> Raj — thanks for the detailed proposal. We're going to pass for now: the revenue-share split doesn't work on our margins.
>
> If you can get to 70/30, I'd genuinely want to look again.
>
> Appreciate the effort you put in.

Why: the "before" reads as a yes and wastes both people's time. The "after" is warm, names the real reason, and leaves a concrete door open.

## Ship checklist

- [ ] One idea, one primary ask.
- [ ] Subject states the content/ask/deadline, under ~50 chars.
- [ ] Opens with context, not "I hope this finds you well" / "I wanted to reach out."
- [ ] The ask is on its own line, specific, with a when.
- [ ] Fits on a phone screen; 50-125 words unless dense info was requested.
- [ ] Register matches the relationship (warm vs curt) and the stakes.
- [ ] If a follow-up: a new reason to reply and an easy out — never "just bumping."
- [ ] Run through write-human (no tells, varied rhythm, plain words).
